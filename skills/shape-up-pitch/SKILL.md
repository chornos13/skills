---
name: shape-up-pitch
description: "Use when writing or assigning work to a team — enforces right-altitude shaping over verbose specs so the team has room to work. Examples: writing a task or ticket, kicking off a project, scoping a feature, reviewing a brief that feels too detailed."
---

Read the rules below, apply them as hard constraints, then handle the user's request.

### The Right Altitude

> "When we shape the work, we need to do it at the right level of abstraction: not too vague and not too concrete."

> "concrete enough that the teams know what to do, yet abstract enough that they have room to work out the interesting details themselves."

Aim for the middle. The work tells the team _what_ must exist and where the edges are — never _how_ to build it.

### Too Fine Is a Failure

> "Work that's too fine, too early commits everyone to the wrong details."

> "When design leaders go straight to wireframes or high-fidelity mockups, they define too much detail too early."

Over-specifying doesn't reduce confusion — it locks the team onto your guesses and removes their judgement. A verbose task is a defect, not diligence.

### Too Vague Also Fails

> "under-specified projects naturally grow out of control because there's no boundary to define what's out of scope."

A one-line task has no edges, so scope creeps. "Room to work" is not "no boundaries" — it's bounded room.

### Boundaries, Not a Spec

> "This isn't a spec. It's more like the boundaries and rules of a game."

Hand over the rules and the field of play. The team decides the moves.

### Leave Details Out — That's What Creates the Room

> "By leaving details out, the breadboard and fat marker methods give room to designers in subsequent phases of the project."

The omission _is_ the gift. Every detail you withhold is a decision you trust the team to make.

### Words, Not Pictures

> "We'll use words for everything instead of pictures."

Describe flows by naming the _places_, the _affordances_ on each, and what _connects_ to what (breadboarding). Words force the right altitude — you physically can't over-specify a layout in prose.

### Pair the Problem With the Solution

> "It's critical to always present both a problem and a solution together."

> "a single specific story that shows why the status quo doesn't work."

Lead with the _why_ as a concrete story. A team that understands the problem makes better calls in the gaps than any spec could dictate.

### Hand Over the Whole Thing — Don't Shred It Into Tasks

> "Splitting the project into tasks up front is like putting the pitch through a paper shredder."

> "The way to really figure out what needs to be done is to start doing real work."

Give the team the whole shaped problem and let _them_ generate the tasks. You define the work; they define the to-dos.

### Room Is Not a Free-for-All

> "Remember: we aren't giving the teams absolute freedom to invent a solution from scratch. We've done the shaping. We've set the boundaries."

The boundaries are the guardrail that makes autonomy safe. Set them deliberately so freedom doesn't become drift.

### Write the No-Gos

> "call out any cases you specifically aren't supporting"

State explicitly what you're _not_ doing. No-gos sharpen the boundary and remove the team's confusion about what isn't theirs to solve.
